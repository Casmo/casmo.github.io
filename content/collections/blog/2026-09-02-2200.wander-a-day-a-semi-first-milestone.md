Pun intended.

As some of you know, we are building a camping tycoon ([Fernweh](https://sparkofchaos.com/fernweh.html)). A cozy/relaxing 'tycoon' game with a emphasize on planning and simulation. These contradict each other but we will make it work. And that requires a lot of thinking, researching, testing and prototyping.

**Our first milestone: timing and beats**
We are working on one of the most crucial parts of the game: how do we make a game interesting where building, growing and maintaining is organically and may take hours, days or even years in game to complete? This is very controversial in modern tycoon games where moving a tree or a building is a matter of a few clicks.

So one of our initial thoughts was: lets have a option to increase the game speed, like Rimworld, where you can toggle between 0-5 different speeds.
This solves the issue that you can now quickly grow (and chop down) trees and build buildings. But lets be real here. If fast forwarding a game is a solution to a boring game, the foundation is broken.

**Lets step back (pun intended). First, some facts:**
- The game has days (including nights). Workers and visitors needs to be able to do a couple of things: sleep, wake up and do a X amount of activities.
- Visitors come and go for multiple days in which they need to reach a certain happiness (one of the goals).
- The game have seasons (unlike some tycoon game, we have all 4, all with different challenges).
- The year consist of 4 seasons.
- A map size is between 200x200m and 2x2km.

So, unless you put one toilet in the corner of a 2km2 map (dont do it Josh), a guest should do roughly the following:
- sleep (rest) 
- eat/drink (hunger)
- toilet (bladder)
- shower (hygiëne)
- 1 or 2 activities (enjoyment)

So around 4-5 things during the day.

Currently we are thinking of around 6 minutes of real time per day, that would give us around 4 minutes of wake time for activities. So each activity may take up to one minute (include walk time) to complete.

If we do some quick Math (these are examples, not final numbers) we *can* end up with 11 hours of real time for one year (at speed 1).
28 days per season (28 x 6 minutes = 168 minutes = 2,8 hours)
4 seasons per year (4 x 2,8 hours = 11,2 hours)

If year is a beat, 11 hours is too long for a game session.

**Shorten the year?**
28 days per season might be too much, what about 20, 10? Or what about having the low seasons have fewer days?
Maybe.

**Shorten the day?**
Risky. If the goal is to have a good ranking of your camping, we can't have guest only use the toilet before going to sleep again.
Not a option.

**Remove the winter?**
Rollercoaster Tycoon has no low seasons, it is skipped. Our game idea is that planning ahead is one of the pillars. I strongly believe that a low season can not only be beautiful to watch (careful, this should not be the gameplay) but also be a real gameplay. While you might have winter campers and heated housing, a winter should give you time to build, maintain and recover facilities and foliage after a high season of travelers.
No, not removing the winter.

**Let's take a look at a few other games.**
- Against the storm: 1 year = 10-12 mins
- Two Point Hospital: 27 min at normal speed
- Rollercoaster Tycoon 2: 55 min at normal speed
- Timberborn: 3 hours per cycle
- RimWorld: 16 hours at base speed
- Stardew Valley: 26 hours without speed controls

I wanna zoom in on RimWorld. It is a game many of our inspiration is coming from: the worker schedule is neat, the worker/building timing seems a nice and I never had real moments that I was bored. But, I used the speed controls often, especially the second or third time I played the game. When you speed up the game two things happen: building is faster, story beat is more cramped.

The difference, that we believe, between RimWorld and Fernweh is that we may attract different type of players to the same game: one who love to build and manage, and some who like to enjoy planning and making everything beautiful. The decorating player might play the game on speed 1 during the whole session. Beats come at a minimum pace and there is plenty to beautify. Do we need to torture the builder in this case, or is speeding up a tool to serve both?

Walk speed, map size, the number of activities per day, the goal and game beats all depend on each other and that is one of the wonderful things of game design.
